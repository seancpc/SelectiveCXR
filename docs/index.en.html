Selective Chest X-ray Interpretation (SelectiveCXR)

Selective Chest X-ray Interpretation with Multi-Source Uncertainty & Conformal Error Control

An AI that says "I'm not sure" · selective accuracy 0.893 after abstention (0.805 answer-all) · Conformal-calibrated error control (≤ α on the calibration distribution) · a single RTX 4090

An AI that knows when to say "I'm not sure." When confident, it reads autonomously; when unsure, it honestly flags the case and defers to a human expert. Validated on chest X-rays (CXR), but the framework is domain-agnostic: the same logic fits defect inspection, financial risk control — any setting where errors are costly and trust must be provable.

Design Concept

This project is not chasing diagnostic accuracy (a red ocean) but trustworthy automation — letting the AI know when it is unreliable and deferring those cases to humans (academically: selective prediction / learning to defer). Three pillars:

  • Selective abstention: read autonomously when confident, actively abstain and defer when not — instead of forcing an answer on every image.
  • Multi-source uncertainty + calibrated error control: quantify uncertainty from the disagreement of three cross-backbone models, then use conformal prediction to control the error rate on auto-read cases at ≤ α under calibration conditions (not guaranteed under data-driven selection or distribution shift — see Known limitations).
  • Paradigm-agnostic: the front-end model is swappable (VLM / CNN / hybrid) while the uncertainty and decision layers stay untouched — the pivot for "medical → cross-domain" transfer.

Overview

Like a good resident: reads most images independently, but knows when to ask the attending physician. Today's medical AI's real danger isn't being wrong — it's being confidently wrong, giving a definitive answer for every image. SelectiveCXR aims for an AI with self-awareness.

  • Task: multi-label classification of 14 CheXpert findings on chest X-rays + a three-way triage decision.
  • Scale: MIMIC-CXR, 1000 calibration + 671 test images, 14 findings, α = 0.15 operating point.

Architecture

Three "differently-born" VLMs (vision-language models) each read the same image; their disagreement serves as the uncertainty signal, and after conformal calibration the study is routed into three tiers.

  • Cross-backbone ensemble: deliberately three different-origin models (Qwen / Gemma / Llama). Architectural diversity → uncorrelated errors → when they "argue," it is a genuinely hard case.
  • Disagreement as signal: the more the three models disagree on a finding, the more likely the system abstains.
  • Conformal Prediction: statistically derives the abstention threshold, controlling the error rate at ≤ α under calibration conditions rather than relying on a hand-tuned threshold.
  • Three-way triage: high-auto / partial-flag / refer-to-human, and it honestly marks the capability boundary (findings it inherently can't judge, always deferred).

Key Results

  • Core selective-prediction validation: after abstaining on the most uncertain samples, accuracy on the auto-read portion clearly beats answering everything (0.893 vs 0.805 answer-all).
  • Capability boundary = 2 / 14: the system marks the two high-prevalence findings that disagreement can't rescue (Cardiomegaly, Lung Opacity) as always-deferred, and runs selective prediction on the rest. (At α=0.15, Atelectasis and Pleural Effusion are also 100% deferred on the test set, so 4 findings are effectively always human-read.)
  • CNN vs VLM (per-label AUC, threshold-free): CNN 0.626 > VLM ensemble 0.585 — the VLM ensemble is slightly below a dedicated CNN (honest, not overclaimed); but the two win and lose per-finding and have uncorrelated errors (CNN stronger on density-type lesions, VLM on morphology-type), which is exactly the basis for a "CNN + VLM hybrid".
  • Multi-label joint abstention (a structural finding): under a strict α=0.05, per-image abstention counts are highly concentrated and "zero abstention over a whole study" almost never happens — revealing a structural challenge of multi-label selective prediction. The demo uses an α=0.15 operating point so all three tiers appear; the true α=0.05 distribution is recorded honestly — a display operating point ≠ hiding the truth.

Risk–Coverage curve (test set, 671 images × 12 findings excluding the 2 capability-boundary findings, 8,052 label-level decisions): x = auto-read fraction, y = error on that subset; closer to the bottom-left is better, with a measured AURC = 0.11. On the curve, keeping error ≤15% allows up to 93% of decisions to be auto-read (an upper bound from ranking test results after the fact; the actual conformal α=0.15 operating point, with thresholds chosen on the calibration set, covers 63.4%); tightening the target to ≤5% collapses coverage to near zero — the system honestly reflects that hard cases cannot even reach 5% error, and defers them rather than faking the target. Method Positioning & Known Limitations

Positioning: the abstention threshold is not hand-tuned — it controls selective risk on a calibration set (error on the auto-read subset ≤ α), placing it in the Conformal Risk Control (CRC) family; an optional Hoeffding upper bound gives a finite-sample guarantee. The closest prior work is AT-CXR, "Uncertainty-Aware Agentic Triage for Chest X-rays" (arXiv 2508.19322).

Known limitations (engaging the frontier debate honestly): recent work argues that "naive conformal prediction should not be used directly for selective classification," for two reasons — (1) conditional coverage: for a data-driven selected subset, standard conformal's marginal guarantee doesn't necessarily transfer; (2) distribution shift: the guarantee degrades under input / prevalence shift. This project controls selective risk directly rather than issuing prediction sets, and tests on out-of-distribution data (NIH, thresholds calibrated on MIMIC) — the system is noticeably more conservative on OOD, matching the safe expectation "when unsure, don't auto-release."

The proper path (future work): upgrade to Conformal Risk Control / Selective Conformal Risk Control, and adopt shift-aware variants (weighted / adaptive conformal) to restore strict guarantees under shift.

Scope & Next

  • CNN + VLM hybrid: the comparison already shows the two have uncorrelated errors and win/lose per-finding — plugging a CNN into the same selective + conformal framework can complement (paradigm-agnostic, the decision layer needs no change).
  • Cross-domain transfer: fits any scenario that simultaneously has high/asymmetric error cost, a human fallback, and a need for provable trust — defect inspection, financial risk control, insurance underwriting; swap chest X-rays for another image domain, only the front-end model changes.

Development Journey

Development wasn't a straight success — it was a series of "hit a problem → validate → honestly correct". A few key moments:

① How does the AI know when it is "unreliable" and should abstain?

Solution: use the disagreement of three cross-backbone models as the uncertainty signal. Validated as effective — after abstaining on the most uncertain samples, accuracy on the auto-read portion reaches 0.893, above the 0.805 of answering everything; at α=0.15 the empirical conformal error rate is 0.107, meeting the "≤ α" target (empirical calibration, coverage 63.4%). Calibrating with the Hoeffding upper bound (δ=0.1) is more conservative: selective accuracy 0.913, error 0.087, coverage 48.6%.

Selective evaluation (empirical, α=0.15): selective accuracy 0.893 vs 0.805 answer-all; empirical conformal error 0.107; coverage 0.634.

② Can the disagreement of lesion localization (grounding) be another signal?

Solution: strict AUC validation showed both cross-model bbox IoU (Definition B, AUC 0.501) and anatomical-model verification (Definition A, AUC 0.463) score about 0.5 (chance level) — no discriminative power. The root cause is that VLMs are reliable at judging but inherently weak at localizing — honestly removed rather than forcing in a dead signal.

Both grounding definitions score an AUC of about 0.5 (Definition B 0.501, Definition A 0.463, chance level); removed after confirming ineffective.

③ The third model kept failing to run — where was the real problem?

Solution: candidate models kept hitting inference-framework (vLLM) version compatibility, not the models themselves. Realizing "the constraint is vLLM, not the model," I ran Llama-3.2-Vision via transformers, restoring the intended cross-backbone design (mixed engines, invisible to the front end).

④ Does a general VLM ensemble's judgment match a dedicated CNN?

Solution: a fair, threshold-free per-label AUC comparison — CNN 0.626 slightly beats the VLM ensemble 0.585, honestly acknowledged; but the two win/lose per-finding and have uncorrelated errors, which becomes the basis for a "CNN + VLM hybrid".

Per-label AUC: CNN (cyan) vs VLM (orange). CNN is slightly higher on average (0.626 vs 0.585), but they win and lose per-finding — VLM is better on Pneumonia, Lung Lesion, Fracture, Lung Opacity — meaning uncorrelated errors.

⑤ Under a strict α=0.05 almost everything is "refer to human" — is the system still usable?

Solution: diagnosed as a structural challenge of multi-label joint abstention (more labels → "zero abstention over a whole study" is harder). Adopted an α=0.15 operating point + a two-boundary triage so all three tiers appear; and honestly marked the 2 capability-boundary findings disagreement can't rescue (threshold −inf, always deferred).

Per-label conformal thresholds: Cardiomegaly and Lung Opacity are −inf (capability boundary, always deferred).
